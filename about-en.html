I'm Gia Han. In every project, I move from a clear question to concrete evidence before drawing a conclusion.

  1. Listen to users, read the market. I put what users say next to market and competitor data to find needs that aren't being met.
  2. Test early with prototypes. Once I have a hypothesis, I build a prototype so people can react right away, then let user feedback decide the next step.
  3. Honest with data. I always separate what has been validated from what is still a hypothesis.

Quick questions

In group projects, which parts were yours?

Furify: I interviewed and took notes with the team, analysed feedback on the Free and Premium plans, and built the website prototype with AI myself. CHẠM: I covered the market opportunity, competitor analysis, value proposition and website prototype; the whole team ran the interviews. Vinamilk: I led the team and worked on market analysis, SWOT–TOWS, segmentation and positioning; teammates came up with the IMC campaign, and I helped reorder its phases.

Have you worked with real users?

Yes, in two group projects. Furify had 10 questionnaires and 7 in-depth interviews with mockup testing. CHẠM had 5 interviews run by the team. Vinamilk and MoMo were desk research, so I say clearly that their conclusions are still hypotheses to be tested.

With samples this small, can the results be trusted?

I treat them as early signals, not conclusions. That is why I report counts out of the total (5/7, 3/7) rather than percentages. The team also set success thresholds upfront so the results could prove us wrong. When an assumption is only partly supported, I say exactly that.

How do you use AI in your work?

I use AI to build website prototypes for Furify and CHẠM, so the team can see and present ideas faster. For Furify user testing, we used a Figma mockup. The asking, listening and analysing are still done by me and the team.
