Furify

An AI pet care app for pet owners aged 20–35 in Ho Chi Minh City.

Team of 5Academic project05/2026
Research goal

Test three assumptions: owners lack nutrition knowledge, would trust AI recommendations, and would pay for Premium.

View website prototype ↗

The prototype appears under the name Pawsitivity AI.

My role

  • Interviewed and took notes with the team
  • Analysed feedback on Free and Premium
  • Built the website prototype with AI

Methods

  • Knowledge questionnaire with 10 pet owners
  • In-depth interviews with 7 owners, Figma mockup test
  • Set success thresholds upfront

Data & tools

  • 10 questionnaires, 7 interviews
  • Low-fidelity Figma mockup
  • Website prototype built with AI

Key results

  • Assumption 2 met: 5/7 willing to use AI
  • Assumption 3 just met: 3/7 willing to pay
  • Assumption 1 only partly supported
Step 1

Assumption 1 was measured with a knowledge questionnaire (n = 10). Assumptions 2 and 3 were measured through in-depth interviews and a Figma mockup test (n = 7).

Reading results against the threshold

The team report concluded that assumption 1 was validated. But against the 60% threshold we set upfront, the result was about 53%, so it only partly supports the assumption. I report it that way here.

Key insight

Owners still prefer a vet for serious issues: “The Free version is useful, but I would rely more on direct veterinary advice.”

What the team changed
  • →Add vet consultation support
  • →Reduce manual data entry
  • →Clarify what makes Premium different
Lesson

Research is there to challenge a solution, not to confirm it. A threshold, once set, has to hold even when the results disappoint. Next time I want to measure real behaviour: usage and Free-to-Premium conversion.

Limitations

Small sample, and the people quoted were all around 20–21, younger than the 20–35 target group. Feedback was based on a mockup, not a real product.
